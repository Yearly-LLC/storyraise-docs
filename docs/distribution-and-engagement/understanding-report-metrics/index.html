Understanding report metrics

Once your report is published and shared, select Analytics in the sidebar and choose the report to see how it's performing. This page walks through every metric you'll find there — what it tells you, why it matters, and what to do about it.

Tip: Want to see each of these on screen first? Take the guided tour of the Analytics page — the real page with sample data, one stop per metric.

Two views: The Numbers and The Story

Analytics has two tabs:

  • The Numbers — every metric, chart, and list, for when you want the detail. That's what the rest of this page covers.
  • The Story — a plain-English summary of your report's performance: what stood out, people worth a follow-up, and ideas for next time. Download it as a PDF to read aloud in a staff meeting or share with your board.

Highlights

The row of short callouts at the top of The Numbers — for example Readers peak on Friday evenings or 65% of visits end after Welcome Letter. Storyraise writes them from your results so you can spot what's worth acting on before you dig into the charts. They only appear once there's enough activity for them to mean something.

Constituents Opened and Constituent Returns

What it tells you: Constituents Opened is how many people on your list opened their personalized link. Constituent Returns is how many of them came back for another visit.

Why it matters: A return visit is one of the strongest signals you get. Someone came back to finish reading, to revisit a story, to show it to a colleague, or to consider taking action.

What to do next: Look for returning names in Most Engaged Constituents and reach out while your report is still fresh in their mind — a returning constituent is a good reason for a timely email or phone call.

Keep in mind:

  • These counts come from personalized links. Readers who arrive through a plain link still count toward Views and the other charts, but they can't be counted as constituents. The two tiles only appear once at least one constituent has opened.
  • A forwarded link counts toward the person you sent it to. Treat a return as a conversation starter, not proof of who did the reading.
  • Don't panic at a low return rate on a short piece. A one-page thank-you is supposed to be read once.

Tip: Keep your own team out of these numbers. Signed-in teammates are excluded automatically, and you can add individual staff addresses or your whole email domain — see Analytics exclusion.

Views

What it tells you: Every visit to your report, including repeat visits from the same reader. Views measure activity, not people. They count visits to the report itself, not email opens.

Why it matters: Views are the clearest read on whether your distribution worked. Email, social media, QR codes, direct mail — this is the number that tells you people arrived.

What to do next: Compare Views with the size of the audience you reached. If activity is lower than expected, the report probably isn't the problem — revisit the subject line, the message, the timing, or where you shared the link.

Avg. Time Spent

What it tells you: Whether readers are spending meaningful time with your report or scanning it quickly. This is the average reader's total time across all of their visits, not per visit.

Why it matters: The number only means something next to your report's length. If your report takes six minutes to read and the average reader spends 40 seconds, they're skimming headlines, not engaging.

What to do next: If readers move through quickly, front-load your impact, shorten the opening letter, and break up longer sections.

Keep in mind:

  • More time is not always better. A long time on a form or donation page may signal confusion rather than enthusiasm. Read this metric alongside How They Read, Section Retention, and Constituent Returns.
  • A visit is recorded after the reader leaves. If someone closes their laptop mid-read or gets interrupted, that session may not be fully captured.

How They Read

Three tiles that show how far people get:

  • Avg. Sections Viewed — how many sections readers typically open.
  • Avg. Time per Section — roughly how long readers spend on each section.
  • Sections in Report — how many sections your report has, for comparison.

If readers view two sections of an eight-section report, most of them never see the back half. Make sure your most important story appears early.

Section Retention

What it tells you: How readers move through your report, which sections hold attention, and where visits end. Each square is a section (bigger means more readers), ribbons show the path to the next section, and the red edge marks where visits stopped. Drag sideways or use the arrow to see later steps.

Why it matters: You can see where your storytelling is working — and where readers lose interest. If half your readers leave after section three, section three is where to tighten.

What to do next: Use those patterns to improve the order, length, and placement of content in your next report. A strong section buried late may deserve a more prominent spot.

Where The Clicks Go

What it tells you: Whether readers took action, and what kind — grouped into Donate, Get Involved, Contact, Watch, Social, Documents, and Other Links. The percentage in the middle is out of everyone who opened the report, including readers who never clicked.

Why it matters: Reading shows attention. Clicking shows intent.

What to do next: Let the click category guide the follow-up. A Donate click may call for a fundraising conversation; a Get Involved click is an opening to share upcoming ways to participate. If few readers click at all, make your call to action easier to find. See Acting on analytics.

When and Where They Visit

What it tells you: When people read your report and the approximate places they read it from.

  • Top Visit Times shows the days and times of day people read, starting from the report's first publish. Use the date range buttons above it to look at the last 7 days up to a full year.
  • Visits Over Time charts visits by day (or by week for longer ranges) and follows the same date range.
  • Where They're Reading maps approximate locations. Select See more for every region and city.

Why it matters: Timing patterns help you choose stronger send times, and geographic patterns can uncover unexpected pockets of interest: alumni, relocated donors, a board member's extended network. Your audience may surprise you — weekday evenings and Sunday afternoons are common reading periods, even though they sit outside traditional email-marketing guidance.

Times are shown in your own time zone, so "Evening" means evening where you are:

Band Hours
Morning 6 to 11am
Midday 11am to 3pm
Afternoon 3 to 6pm
Evening 6pm to midnight
Overnight Midnight to 6am

What to do next: Look for patterns across several weeks or months rather than reacting to one day. Use timing trends to schedule future sends, spikes in Visits Over Time to see which outreach worked, and geographic clusters to identify opportunities for regional events, local volunteer groups, or targeted outreach.

Keep in mind: Location data is approximate. Internet providers, mobile networks, corporate networks, and VPNs all affect city-level accuracy, so focus on broader regional patterns rather than individual cities.

Most Engaged Constituents (personalized reports only)

What it tells you: Which named readers stood out on this specific report, based on attention, depth, and action. Storyraise gives each a zero- to three-star Engagement Score and may add intent badges for actions like clicking a donation link. Select See more for every constituent, with their reading time, how much of the report they read, sections, sessions, and link clicks.

This list only appears when readers arrive through personalized links. A report shared with a plain public link records all the same activity, but there are no names to attach it to — so no stars, no badges, and no constituent list.

Why it matters: These signals help you prioritize timely, personal outreach.

What to do next: Treat highly engaged readers as a stewardship call list — but use the score alongside what you already know about each constituent. Stars reflect engagement with one report, not a person's overall value or relationship with your organization.

The Storyraise Engagement Score

A 0 to 3 star rating, applied to identified constituents only. The benchmark is the median reading time among identified constituents on that report — so the score is always relative to how everyone else read that same piece. Select How it works on the card for a summary.

Rating Name Earned when
0 stars None Below the median reading time for this report. Not a bad reader — just not a standout on this piece.
★ Attention Total reading time is above the median for this report.
★★ Depth Above median, and got through at least half the report. For a multi-section report that means at least half the sections opened; for a single-section report it's measured by sustained reading time.
★★★ Intent Above median, at least half the report consumed, and a follow-up action such as a link click or a return visit.

Intent badges are awarded independently of stars. A reader who spent ten seconds but clicked "Donate Now" still shows a Donation badge. That's deliberate: a meaningful action is never hidden behind a time threshold. Badges include Clicked Donation CTA, Clicked Event RSVP, Clicked Volunteer, Watched YouTube, and Revisited Report.

A note on what the numbers don't include

Your own team's views are automatically excluded, so these metrics reflect your real audience — see Analytics exclusion.

If you're looking at a report first published before late May 2026, expect a smaller set of metrics: see Analytics for older reports.
