Donor Signals

Know who needs you this week.

Donor Signals scores every donor you have, every day. It sorts them into six groups, tells you why each person is in front of you, and gives you something to make for them.

    What Donor Signals does

    Every donor scored overnight, grouped by what they need next.

    Six groups, and what each is worth

    New gifts, upgrade candidates, warming up, highly engaged, at risk, and stable. Under every count is the money behind it, so you can see which group is worth more of your week.

    Every row says why now

    No gift in four months, and they usually give every two. Then what you already know about them: what they have given, and whether they read what you send.

    Something to make for them

    Each group comes with two things you can make: a video recorded once for everyone in it, or a report written for their situation. The group travels with you into the builder.

    At risk means late for them, not late on a calendar

    A monthly donor who has missed three gifts is late. An annual donor who gave in November is not.

    What most tools do

    • Pick a number of days, usually 365, and call everyone past it lapsed.
    • Bury your monthly donors, who are three months late and look fine.
    • Flag your annual donors every spring, when nothing is wrong.

    One rule for donors who behave nothing alike.

    What Donor Signals does

    • Takes the median gap between each donor's own gifts.
    • Flags them when they are past their own rhythm, not yours.
    • Orders the list by what is at stake, so the donor you would least like to lose is first.

    A donor with one gift on file has no rhythm to measure, so the calendar still applies to them.

    Reading is the part no other system has. Storyraise publishes the reports, so it knows who opened one and how often. In a lapsed group, the donors who stopped giving and still read you are the winnable ones.

    From a list to something made

    A week with Donor Signals

    Five things worth doing, in the order most teams find useful.
    1. 1
      Thank the new gifts first. Everyone who gave in the last fortnight, before the week gets away from you. One recorded thank-you covers all of them.
    2. 2
      Work the top of the at-risk list. It leads with the money. Start with the donors who stopped giving and still open your reports.
    3. 3
      Make the ask where it will land. Upgrade candidates are giving more and reading more. Highly engaged read everything you publish and are rarely asked for anything.
    4. 4
      Give the warming-up group something to read. They are paying more attention than they were a month ago. Keep the attention you have earned.
    5. 5
      Read Giving health once a month. Retention by gift size is the table that changes plans. It shows which band of donors is worth keeping and which is leaking.
