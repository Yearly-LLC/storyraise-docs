# Analytics for older reports

Reports first published before **late May 2026** were measured with Storyraise's earlier page-view counter. Their history is preserved, but they won't show the full set of newer Analytics features — so a report you published last year can look sparse next to one you published last month.

## What older reports still show

- **Lifetime Opens** — the running total the old counter recorded.
- **A regional reading summary** — approximate geography for the readers it saw.
- **Legacy constituent engagement** — the identified readers it captured.
- **The Story** — the plain-English summary, which is written to work with whatever data exists.

If an older report's Analytics tab looks empty, **The Story** tab is the place to go. It's built for exactly this case.

## What they may not show

The newer measurements simply weren't being recorded at the time, so they can't be backfilled:

- Unique Visitors and Return Visitors
- Average Time Spent
- Detailed click activity by category
- Section Retention and Reader Flow
- Visit timing (the When They Visit heatmap)
- The star-rated Engagement Score

## Figures labeled "estimated"

Where The Story needs a number the old counter never captured, it may present a figure labeled **estimated**. Treat these as helpful indicators, not direct measurements — they're useful for a sense of scale, but don't quote them as precise results in a board report.

## Don't compare old Opens with new Opens

> **They're counted differently.** The old counter incremented on every page load; the new engine de-duplicates repeat loads from the same reader. An older report's Opens will look inflated next to a newer report's — that's a change in measurement, not in performance.

Compare newer reports with other newer reports. For year-over-year storytelling, lean on qualitative comparisons or on metrics both eras share, and note the methodology change wherever the numbers appear side by side.

If you have questions about a specific older report — or you'd like us to look at whether more of its history can be recovered — [contact the Storyraise team](https://storyraise.com/support).
