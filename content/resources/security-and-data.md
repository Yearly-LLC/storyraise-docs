# Security and data governance

If you're evaluating Storyraise for an organization that holds donor records, this page is the starting point — what the product does with constituent data, what's documented, and what to request from us directly.

> **Formal documentation.** Our subprocessor list is published in our [GDPR statement](https://storyraise.com/gdpr). For a Data Processing Agreement, security documentation, or a completed vendor security questionnaire, [contact our support team](https://storyraise.com/support) and say what your review requires. These are provided on request rather than published here.

## What Storyraise holds

When you connect a CRM or import a file, Storyraise stores the constituent records you bring in — names, email addresses, and whichever other fields you choose to bring across — in your organization's constituent list. It also stores engagement generated by your reports: which constituents opened them, and what they did there. See [Tracking engagement](../distribution-and-engagement/tracking-engagement.md).

The scope is set by you. Storyraise imports the fields you map; if a field shouldn't leave your CRM, don't sync it.

## Direction of data flow

Every integration is **read-only by default** — Storyraise reads from your system of record and does not modify it.

The exceptions are [Salesforce](../crm-and-data/integrations/salesforce.md) and [Raiser's Edge NXT](../crm-and-data/integrations/blackbaud-raisers-edge-nxt.md), which offer optional engagement write-back. When you enable and configure it, Storyraise can log a completed Task on a Salesforce Contact or an action on a Raiser's Edge NXT constituent, or update custom fields you map — one entry per person, kept up to date as their engagement changes. Write-back is off until you turn it on, and every other integration is read-only.

See [Connecting a CRM](../crm-and-data/connecting-a-crm.md).

## Access inside your organization

Access is granted **per area, not per field**. Anyone with View access to Constituents can see every field on every constituent, including any giving data you've synced. There is no field-level restriction, so the practical control is what you choose to sync and who you add to your team.

Folder restrictions filter what people see in the reports list, but as [Report folders](../getting-started/report-folders.md) states plainly, that is not a security control.

Review [User roles and permissions](../getting-started/user-roles-and-permissions.md) before adding anyone, and note that [single sign-on](../account-and-settings/single-sign-on.md) creates accounts on first login without an invitation — so whoever your identity provider lets through is in, unless you've removed them from your team.

## What your readers can see

A [personalized link](../distribution-and-engagement/personalized-links.md) carries a constituent's email address in the URL and renders that person's merged fields. Anyone who receives a forwarded link sees that data. Don't merge restricted fields into a report you distribute this way, and treat personalized links as confidential correspondence.

Published reports are hidden from search engines by default, but as [Publishing and sharing](../getting-started/publishing-and-sharing.md) says, that is not the same as private — anyone with the link can open it.

## Deleting data

Disconnecting an integration optionally deletes the collections it synced, along with any constituents it was the only source for. This can't be undone. See [Managing connections](../crm-and-data/managing-connections.md).

Deleting a Storyraise Collect response also removes any file uploaded with it: within about an hour the file's link stops working, and after a 30-day window in which support can restore it, the file is permanently deleted. See [Managing responses](../storyraise-collect/managing-responses.md).

## Hosting and encryption

- **Where data lives:** Storyraise runs on Google Cloud in the United States. Customer data, backups, and replicas stay in US locations.
- **In transit:** all connections use TLS 1.2 or higher.
- **At rest:** data is encrypted with AES-256 using Google-managed keys.
